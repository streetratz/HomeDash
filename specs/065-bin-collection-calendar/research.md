# Official-Source Research — Bin Collection Calendar

**Reviewed**: 2026-10-10, Australia UTC+11  
**Issue**: [streetratz/HomeDash #19](https://github.com/streetratz/HomeDash/issues/19)  
**Purpose**: Inform behaviour and acceptance cases, not publish an authoritative
schedule for any household or country.

## Evidence Boundaries

Research used live web search/open tools against council or municipal-provider
sources. Dunedin's two 2026 calendar PDFs were also visually inspected. No
household address was supplied to a lookup service. Public search terms contained
only council names and collection topics, not repository code or private data.

The owner's preset is a **product preference**: red General waste weekly, yellow
Recycling fortnightly on week A, green Garden waste fortnightly on week B.
It is not an AU/NZ standard. Local service, suburb, housing type, legacy lid
colours, organics definitions, and holidays vary. Garden-only waste and combined
food/garden organics (FOGO) must not be assumed equivalent.

“Reviewed” means the evidence was retrieved during this session, not that every
published calendar remains current. Historical notices and search-index-only
evidence are labelled below. No 2026/27 seasonal service is inferred from a
2025/26 notice. Householders must confirm actual dates with their provider.

## Evidence-to-Scope Decisions

These are specification decisions/inferences, not claims that councils recommend
HomeDash product behaviour.

1. **Preset versus authority**: Keep the owner's explicit requested preset and
   explain its customisable status. AU2/AU3/NZ2 show materially different patterns.
2. **Independent schedules**: Include weekday sets and N-week date-anchored cycles.
   AU3 demonstrates different weekdays; Philadelphia/Paris demonstrate multi-day
   and daily needs. Dunedin's opposing zones show why a known date is necessary.
3. **Holiday handling**: Manual skip/replace/add dates are included. Brisbane
   continues public-holiday service; Auckland's historical calendar shifts dates;
   Dunedin targets specific holidays. A universal holiday algorithm is unsafe.
4. **Seasonal/irregular boundary**: Explicit dates are included; automatic monthly,
   short-month, and seasonal rule editors are deferred. NZ3 and historical Cambridge
   motivate capability but do not establish this owner's exact seasonal choices.
5. **Household date correctness**: Date-only obligations and an explicitly confirmed
   household timezone define display/rollover. This is a correctness decision, not
   a council instruction about software timezone conversion.
6. **Conservative draft defaults**: Existing-calendar display, one household, and
   manual entry are assumed limits for this draft. The issue's dedicated-widget,
   multi-property, and bin-import possibilities are neither silently approved nor
   promised; scope can be changed before planning.

## Research Limitations and Failures

- Brisbane technical-notes PDF and Bega Valley page opens returned internal fetch
  errors; indexed official text is explicitly labelled rather than called full-page
  verification.
- Seattle's page open failed twice; the council search index corroborated the
  single-family frequency statement. No other site's claim was substituted.
- Auckland calendar text was accessible but its screenshot was blocked as a
  restricted URL. No interpretation of its visual shaded week boundaries is used.
- Christchurch production collection-page retrieval was inconsistent; staging
  (`uat`) results and community posts were not relied on for policy.
- Search did not establish a newer authoritative Auckland calendar or 2026/27
  seasonal notice for Thames-Coromandel/Cambridge. Historical findings remain
  useful counterexamples but are not current household instructions.
- Holiday examples do not imply automatic adjustment or live council connectivity.
  All entered dates are the administrator's responsibility to verify.

## Planning Technical Research — 2026-10-10 UTC+11

This appendix adds implementation decisions only. All council-source evidence,
provenance, historical qualifications and earlier evidence-to-scope decisions
above are preserved. The earlier draft-default wording records the specification
phase; CH-02 subsequently confirmed Calendar-only icon-and-text presentation.
No new council policy, address lookup or implied owner approval is added.
Direct local research (no delegated agents) follows the bounded planning request.
No repository code/private data was submitted to public searches.

### Decision 1 — Separate authoritative collections from provider events

**Decision**: New singleton/stream/date/exception tables; pure read-only range
generation and a typed collection branch inside existing Calendar UI.

**Rationale**: Current `backend/src/services/calendar-sync-service.ts`
materializes/prunes events to a limited sync window and defaults unknown local
source handling to birthdays. `backend/src/api/admin-calendar.ts` applies one
source colour, limits queries to 200 rows and auto-enables new sources in all
Calendar widgets. These do not preserve independent stream/date semantics or
explicit visibility without invasive changes.

**Alternatives considered**: New provider type with materialized events (window/
sync/colour/public coupling); synthetic ICS (improper irregular-exception and
ownership coupling); stream definitions in widget JSON (duplicate household
state, poor backup/identity and transaction semantics); dedicated widget
(owner explicitly excluded). Separate persistent state and display integration
is the smallest safe adaptation. Existing imports/birthdays remain unchanged.

### Decision 2 — Civil-date ordinal engine plus explicit household date context

**Decision**: Strict Gregorian keys, signed Monday-week offset modulo interval,
bounded direct range iteration; Intl named timezone to derive household today.
Agenda GET uses server-derived `daysAhead`; fullscreen uses explicit date keys.
No new date/recurrence dependency.

**Rationale**: `frontend/src/lib/calendarDates.ts` distinguishes old UTC-midnight
all-day events from timed browser dates but does not establish household-relative
Today. `CalendarWidget.tsx` and `FullScreenCalendar.tsx` memoize browser-local
date context; `AgendaView.tsx` uses browser Today. New collection date path avoids
changing original provider behavior, handles anchors before/after range, and
does not iterate real 24-hour DST instants.

**Alternatives considered**: Annual odd/even ISO weeks (year-boundary phase
changes); browser local or server local today (different visitor dates);
timezone-midnight timestamp expansion (DST ambiguity); RRULE/monthly abstraction
(outside manual/N-week scope and unnecessary dependency).

Primary reference for future implementation verification: ECMA-402 Intl.DateTimeFormat's named timezone,
Gregorian calendar and parts API:
`https://tc39.es/ecma402/#sec-intl.datetimeformat`
(lookup attempted during planning, but no usable fetched evidence was retained).
Local code/runtime API context informs this design; verify parts/date behavior
with the planned tests rather than treating the unsuccessful lookup as evidence.
The chosen civil-date recurrence convention is the feature specification's rule,
not a standards requirement or a new council fact.

### Decision 3 — Admin aggregate save and complete collision validation

**Decision**: Strict bounded full household draft, preview/digest, optimistic
persisted revision plus runtime epoch, one immediate SQLite transaction.
Validate every exception origin/destination, even outside the displayed range.
Stable stream/exception UUIDs and explicit destructive confirmations.

**Rationale**: Small one-household scope makes a complete draft simpler than
interdependent CRUD operations. Existing `auth/requireRole.ts` has administrator
membership checks and `auth/csrf.ts` has synchronizer-token enforcement.
`db/sqlite.ts` already enables WAL, foreign keys and bounded busy timeout.
Stale-draft handling prevents unrelated edits from being overwritten and runtime
epoch invalidates pre-restore drafts without changing restored persisted data.

**Alternatives considered**: Best-effort partial saves (spec prohibits);
last-writer-wins (lost updates); collision checks only in preview (hidden future
conflicts); phase-shifting replacement (spec prohibits); durable preview job/
token table (unneeded state/infrastructure).

Primary references for future implementation verification: SQLite transaction and foreign-key documentation:
`https://sqlite.org/lang_transaction.html` and
`https://sqlite.org/foreignkeys.html` (lookup attempted; no usable fetched evidence
retained). Local SQLite configuration and restore code were inspected directly.
Immediate transaction acquisition
can fail busy; semantic failure must roll back, and FK structure alone cannot
validate computed recurrence collisions. These technical sources do not imply
any generated migration or application test ran.

### Decision 4 — Widget-scoped reads and explicit collection public subset

**Decision**: Separate admin config, authenticated widget display and sole new
public GET inside `api/public.ts`. Server resolves selected device-context
dashboard/widget and `publicCollectionStreamIds` subset, returning an allowlisted
display DTO with no private rule/provider state.

**Rationale**: Legacy public Calendar sources/events in `admin-calendar.ts`
are not widget-scoped, and `calendarHooks.ts` calls the public event route.
`publicVisibility.ts` treats Calendar as intrinsic public; generic explicit-
widget snapshot resolver/loader supports other types. New collection tables
cannot be enumerated by legacy provider routes. Collection-specific public
selection defaults empty and is independent of old provider visibility.
Current `api/user.ts` authenticated dashboard read uses requireAuth, not a
general ACL helper: do not invent or claim enforcement of unused ACL tables.

**Alternatives considered**: Reusing loose public events/source listing (leaks
new household state); assuming all intrinsic calendars publish collections
(implicit new publication); adding Calendar to unrelated snapshot union (wider
behavior refactor); public requests supplying stream/user IDs (enumeration).
No global calendar-public rewrite is needed in this bounded feature.

### Decision 5 — Additive generated migration and portable backup v2

**Decision**: Four new Drizzle-generated tables, no default preset seeding,
backup arrays/table map/FK-safe ordering, portable version 2 with v1 restore
support and pre-destructive collection semantic validation.

**Rationale**: `backupTypes.ts` currently uses version 1 with record-array
schemas and excludes derived calendarEvents; `backupService.ts` has explicit
TABLE_MAP/INSERT_ORDER/reversed deletion and a restore transaction. Simply
adding optional arrays to version 1 lets older readers silently discard them.
Version 2 makes unsupported restores reject clearly; v1 full replacement warns
that it has no collections. `db/migrate.ts` already handles generated FK guards/
checks; no migration-runner change or table rebuild is justified.

**Alternatives considered**: Reuse event backup (derived events intentionally
excluded); broad record-only validation (malformed date/rules can enter state);
version 1 additive optional fields (silent loss on old readers); handwritten SQL
(repository prohibits). Recovery uses a pre-upgrade DB backup with matching
prior image, not an assumed down-migration.

### Decision 6 — Scoped UI, safety bounds and testing

**Decision**: Existing CalendarsPanel manager and CalendarConfigForm selection,
shared collection group rows in agenda/fullscreen, no decorative dense-list
motion. Limits 32 streams, 2,048 explicit dates/stream, 512 exceptions/regular
stream, 8,192 total entries, 2 MiB input, 731-day range and supported years
1900–2199; these are justified implementation safety choices.

**Rationale**: Existing frontend is React 18/TanStack Query/Radix/Tailwind, not
stale Fastify4/Node20/pnpm8 inventory. Existing useWidgetVisibility can gate
≤30s collection queries; useAdaptivePoll is Sonos-specific and not repurposed.
Visible names, distinct date context and complete next-date groups satisfy CH-02
without a new widget or general calendar redesign.
Positive+negative permission, date/exception, midnight/DST, backup/upgrade/offline
and existing-provider tests are mandatory future evidence.

**Alternatives considered**: New UI/framework/dependencies, repeated config per
widget, unbounded ranges/finite lists, hiding names in compact chips or applying
one source colour, provider maxEvents cutting collection groups, assuming an
automated test proves human usability. All are inappropriate to this scope.
